# Course information

## AC215 / E115: Productionizing AI

Harvard AC215/E115 covers the full lifecycle of AI systems, with a strong emphasis on large language models. Students design, build, deploy, and operate a complete application. Topics connect modeling to data pipelines, containers, cloud infrastructure, training, inference, evaluation, interfaces, and software delivery.

By the end of the course, students should be able to explain MLOps, LLMOps, and AIOps; deploy and scale AI models; build reproducible data and training workflows; and operate a usable AI application.

Enrollment is closed; the class has reached capacity. [Watch Prof. Pavlos Protopapas's introductory video on Canvas](https://canvas.harvard.edu/courses/170012); course access may be required.

## Prerequisites

Students should be comfortable with:

- Python functions, classes, modules, lists, dictionaries, and file input/output.
- NumPy, Pandas, and Matplotlib.
- Basic deep learning in TensorFlow or PyTorch, including training a simple model and handling data.
- Basic machine-learning concepts, training, and evaluation.
- Command-line navigation, scripts, and basic file operations.

An introductory machine-learning course with Python programming is a useful preparation. Students are expected to learn quickly and work independently.

The project accounts for 82% of the course grade. There is no in-class midterm or final exam.

Six short quizzes assess recent material through conceptual questions and short coding tasks. Quizzes normally last 20–30 minutes during class. Only the best four count; students can miss up to two without penalty. There are no make-ups. On-campus students take quizzes in person, with no online option. DCE students take quizzes online.

## Deadlines and late days

All milestones are due at 10:00 PM ET on their listed dates.

Each student has four late days total for MS1–MS4, with at most two late days on one milestone. A group submission uses the same number of late days for every member. For example, one day late uses one day from each member's allowance. Days cannot be transferred, pooled, or borrowed between students or groups.

MS5 has no late days and no extensions. For serious medical or personal emergencies affecting other deadlines, contact staff as early as possible, preferably before the deadline. Staff review these cases individually; if advance contact is impossible, contact staff as soon as you can.

## Academic honesty and collaboration

Discussion, office hours, debugging with peers, and properly cited small code snippets are allowed. Submitted work must reflect your own understanding. Do not access or share solutions before submission, plagiarize, omit attribution, pay for coursework, or share course materials with future students.

AI tools may support learning and development. Every student must understand, explain, and defend all project code and design choices, including AI-assisted code. Inability to explain it during evaluation can reduce the grade. All team members must understand the full system, regardless of how work is divided.

Course materials are intended primarily for enrolled students and personal educational use. Do not sell, commercialize, distribute, publish, or create derivatives of course material without instructor permission. Permitted use must acknowledge the instructor, course, and institution.

## Auditing

Request audit permission by emailing ac215harvard@gmail.com with your HUID and agreement to the auditing terms. You must not be enrolled when requesting audit status; students listed in Canvas must drop before being added as auditors.

Auditors must attend in person and confirm attendance during the first week. Academic-honesty rules apply. Auditors cannot later take the course for credit, submit coursework or milestones, participate in course projects, use Ed Discussion, or attend TF support hours.
